Practical Guide

Eating Well in Tokyo on a Budget

Tokyo has a reputation for expensive dining, but some of the best meals in the city cost less than a coffee in New York. Here's what each price level actually gets you.

The good news: Tokyo's dining scene spans every budget, and the best meal of your trip might be a ¥900 bowl of ramen rather than a ¥30,000 kaiseki. Our directory lists 33 restaurants across three price bands — here's what to expect at each level, and which restaurants to try.

$ · Under ¥1,000 — the everyday meal

This is where Tokyo truly shines. A full, satisfying meal for under ¥1,000 is not a compromise — it's the heart of Japanese food culture. At this level you get:

  • Ramen, udon, and soba — a hearty bowl of noodles, often with toppings, for ¥800–1,000.
  • Japanese curry — a rich curry rice plate, often with pork cutlet, for ¥800–1,200.
  • Fast-casual sushi — conveyor-belt (kaiten) sushi where most plates are ¥100–300.
  • Rice bowls (donburi) — gyudon (beef bowl), katsudon (pork cutlet bowl) at chains like Yoshinoya or Matsuya for ¥400–700.

From our directory: Afuri (yuzu ramen), Ippudo Roppongi (tonkotsu ramen), Curry Bondy (beef curry), Udon Shin and Marugame Seimen (udon), and Honke Owariya (soba).

$$ · ¥1,000–3,000 — the sweet spot

This is the most rewarding band for visitors. For ¥1,500–3,000 per person you can eat very well:

  • Izakaya meals — several shared small plates, a drink, and a full stomach for ¥2,000–3,000 per person.
  • Okonomiyaki and monjayaki — a savory pancake you grill at the table, ¥1,000–1,500.
  • Mid-range sushi — a proper sushi lunch set at a reputable counter, ¥2,000–3,000.
  • Set meals (teishoku) — a balanced plate with rice, soup, and a main, ¥1,000–1,500.

From our directory: Sushi Dai (market omakase, ~¥5,000 — slightly above this band but famous for its value), Pokemon Cafe (themed cafe), Ginza Mizuno (okonomiyaki), and Tamawarai (soba).

$$$ · ¥3,000 and up — the splurge

This is Tokyo's world-famous high-end dining. It's expensive, but often remarkable value compared to Michelin-starred dining in other world cities:

  • Counter sushi (omakase) — a full chef's-choice course at a top sushi counter, ¥15,000–30,000.
  • Wagyu & yakiniku — A5-grade Japanese beef grilled at your table, ¥10,000–20,000.
  • Kaiseki — a multi-course haute-cuisine dinner, ¥20,000–40,000+.
  • Tempura — a dedicated tempura counter, ¥15,000–30,000.

From our directory: Sushi Koshikawa and Shinjuku Sushi Hatsume (high-end sushi), the Ginza wagyu houses (Ginza Yakiniku Salon de Aging Beef, Beef Professional Ginza, Akita Beef Teppanyaki), and Waketokuyama (kaiseki).

Tips for eating well on a budget

  • Lunch is the deal. Many high-end restaurants offer lunch sets at a third to half the dinner price. A ¥20,000 kaiseki dinner might have a ¥7,000 lunch.
  • Look for teishoku (set meals). The set meal — rice, soup, main, pickles — is Japan's best-value format and everywhere at lunch.
  • Department store food halls (depachika) sell high-quality prepared food and bento at bargain prices, especially in the evening.
  • Standing bars and counter seats are cheaper than table service, and often the same quality.
  • Cash is still king at small, cheap shops — bring yen, not just a card.
