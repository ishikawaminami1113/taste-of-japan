Neighborhood Guide

Where to Eat in Tokyo: 18 Neighborhoods

Tokyo doesn't have one dining scene — it has one per neighborhood. Here's what each area in our directory does best, and how to browse the restaurants there.

Each Tokyo neighborhood keeps its own dining character — the counters of Ginza, the ramen alleys of Shinjuku, the market sushi of Toyosu. The directory currently lists 33 restaurants across the 18 areas below. Tap an area name to see its listings pre-filtered; combine it with the cuisine filter to narrow things further.

Ginza 9 restaurants

Tokyo's most famous upscale district — luxury boutiques, department-store food halls, and polished counters. Our Ginza listings lean refined: four wagyu and teppanyaki houses (including Ginza Yakiniku Salon de Aging Beef and Akita Beef Teppanyaki Ginza Gomei), a tempura restaurant, the long-standing soba house Honke Owariya, a classic curry chain, Botejyu for okonomiyaki, and Ginza Mizuno for okonomiyaki since 1947. Many are a short walk from Ginza Station.

Shinjuku 6 restaurants

The city's busiest hub, where neon office towers meet ramen alleys and izakaya lanes. Our Shinjuku picks span sushi counters, two ramen shops — Fuunji and Ichiran Ramen Shinjuku — an izakaya, and two udon houses (Udon Shin and Marugame Seimen). A good cross-section for a first night in town.

Browse restaurants in Shinjuku →

Shibuya 2 restaurants

Famous for the scramble crossing and youth culture, but also a serious dining neighborhood of counter sushi and casual izakayas. Our Shibuya listings are Sushi Inase and the izakaya Fry-ya, both a short walk from the station.

Browse restaurants in Shibuya →

Ebisu 2 restaurants

A relaxed neighborhood built around Ebisu Garden Place, with a mix of offices, homes, and quiet restaurants. Our listings are Ebisu Sushi Hatsume, a counter sushi restaurant, and Afuri, the ramen shop famous for its yuzu-infused broth.

Browse restaurants in Ebisu →

Toyosu 1 restaurant

Home to the famous Toyosu fish market, successor to Tsukiji. Our listing is Sushi Dai, the legendary market sushi counter known for its ¥5,000 omakase — widely called the best-value omakase in Tokyo. Seats are assigned by signup at the storefront from 5 AM.

Browse restaurants in Toyosu →

Roppongi 1 restaurant

An international district of embassies, art museums, and nightlife. Our listing is Ippudo Roppongi, the Hakata tonkotsu ramen chain's late-night branch — open until 2 AM on weekdays, with an English menu and pictures.

Browse restaurants in Roppongi →

Jimbocho 1 restaurant

Tokyo's book town, full of used bookstores and old-school eateries. Our listing is Curry Bondy, a tiny decades-old curry shop beloved for its rich, slow-simmered beef curry — cash only, with a loyal lunch line.

Browse restaurants in Jimbocho →

Nihonbashi 1 restaurant

A historic merchant district and transport hub, home to the flagship Mitsukoshi department store. Our listing is the official Pokemon cafe, with character-themed dishes in a bright, playful room — reservations open 31 days ahead and sell out fast.

Browse restaurants in Nihonbashi →

Nishi-Azabu 1 restaurant

A quiet, upscale residential area near Roppongi, known for embassies and fine dining. Our listing is Tamawarai, a celebrated soba restaurant whose chef grinds his own buckwheat — a Michelin fixture for years, with waits of two hours or more.

Browse restaurants in Nishi-Azabu →

Harajuku 1 restaurant

Takeshita Street's sweets and fashion, plus stylish restaurants tucked into the side streets. Our Harajuku listing is the famously colorful Kawaii Monster Cafe — a loud, playful dinner show rather than a quiet meal.

Browse restaurants in Harajuku →

Akihabara 1 restaurant

Electric Town: anime, manga, arcade culture, and themed cafes. Our Akihabara listing is Maid Cafe @home cafe, part of the neighborhood's well-known maid-cafe scene. Come for the atmosphere as much as the food.

Browse restaurants in Akihabara →

Ikebukuro 1 restaurant

A major hub in the north, home to Sunshine City and its own lively food streets. Our Ikebukuro listing is Dohtonbori, an okonomiyaki house — savory pancakes cooked at the table.

Browse restaurants in Ikebukuro →

Akasaka 1 restaurant

A business district of polished offices, embassies, and high-end sushi. Our Akasaka listing is Sushi Koshikawa, a counter sushi restaurant in the calm backstreets near Akasaka Station.

Browse restaurants in Akasaka →

Azabu-Juban 1 restaurant

A quiet, upscale residential area with a long shopping street, traditional sweets, and weekend markets. Our listing is Soba Tokyo Sarashina Horii, a soba restaurant a short walk from Azabu-Juban Station.

Browse restaurants in Azabu-Juban →

Daikanyama 1 restaurant

A leafy, fashion-forward neighborhood near Shibuya, known for its cafe culture and low-rise streets. Our listing is Daikanyama Issai Kassai, an izakaya-style spot with an all-you-can-eat format.

Browse restaurants in Daikanyama →

Ningyocho 1 restaurant

A historic business district near Tokyo Station, full of long-established shops and depachika (department-store food halls). Our listing is Waketokuyama, a kaiseki restaurant in the old downtown quarter.

Browse restaurants in Ningyocho →

Tsukishima 1 restaurant

An island neighborhood famous for monjayaki — a Tokyo-style savory pancake — and its packed Monja Street. Our Tsukishima listing is Hikarimono, a sushi counter, for something quieter.

Browse restaurants in Tsukishima →

Yoyogi-Uehara 1 restaurant

A calm residential area by Yoyogi Park and Meiji Shrine, a short ride from Harajuku. Our listing is Tsuta, a celebrated ramen shop that made headlines as the first ramen restaurant to earn a Michelin star.

Browse restaurants in Yoyogi-Uehara →
